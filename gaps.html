02

Open questions

Six decisions that are not ours to make. Each one blocks copy that is otherwise written and ready.

Operations

Does a runner scan at a host counter, or is the code the whole hand-over there too?

On the sender side the code <i>is</i> the hand-over — there is no scan action, and the sender copy has been corrected. But the host review document asks for the opposite: its H1b email is to explain “how to verify a runner, scan every parcel, release the handover code only after scanning”. Either hosts scan and senders do not, or the host copy is describing a step that does not exist. Until this is settled the host and runner lanes still say “after the scan”.

Product

Does sender sign-in move to a device PIN, the way the runner app is doing?

The runner review retires the per-sign-in code SMS in favour of a PIN after the first sign-up code. The same argument applies to senders, and it is the single biggest SMS saving available. The site currently keeps the sender code SMS until this is decided.

Operations

If a host does not answer a dwell-extension request before the deadline, what happens?

Auto-approve, auto-decline or escalate to ops. The receiver-facing approval and denial messages already exist, so the copy for H7 cannot be finished until the default is set.

Operations

Is automatic redelivery the approved default when a host does not respond to a failed delivery?

H3 tells the host what happens if they do nothing. If that sentence is wrong, it is worse than having no sentence.

Finance

Does settlement actually reach a host within one working day?

H4 puts the amount in the subject line and the timing in the body. A settlement promise we miss costs more than a vaguer one — change the sentence, not the finance process.

Commercial

What are the real pod-host commercial terms?

H6 is currently inactive and the draft promises 2 m², installation, maintenance and insurance. It must state the approved fee structure rather than implying unspecified earnings, and none of it ships until the terms are confirmed.

The team

The receiver lane was not in the review pack.

Senders, runners and hosts came back marked. The receiver changes on this site — splitting the created notification from the moving parcel, and dropping the 6-hour rung — come from the brief rather than from the team, and still need the same keep/remove pass.

03

Four weeks

Week 1

Stop the bleeding

  • C4c — Fix the {{station}} token printing where the collection code belongs — receivers on the paid rung are getting the wrong value.
  • R7 — Add a collapse key to FcmSender and key offers on batch_offer:{batch_id}.
  • R7 — Restore the proximity and vehicle-class filters in BatchOffer::Build; stop pushing offers to runners who are offline.
  • S2d — Retire one of parcel_dropped / parcel_handed_to_runner.
  • Normalise currency to Ksh across all 33 live templates.
  • R8b — Retire the per-sign-in code SMS in favour of a device PIN — the single biggest SMS saving on the estate, and the review has already approved it.
Week 2

Rewrite what already fires

  • Replace the body of every live template with the copy in the library. No schema change, no new triggers — it is a content migration against NotificationTemplate keys.
  • R7 — Put payout, stop count and distance in the offer title.
  • C4a — Retire the 6-hour dwell reminder; the ladder becomes four rungs.
  • C4a C4b C4c — Re-time the ladder onto the 24-hour storage clock. The seeded keys dwell_reminder_24h, _48h and _storage_started_72h describe a 72-hour free window that does not exist — the names become misleading the day the schedule changes.
  • H4 — Move the settlement amount into the email subject line.
  • Apply the review's channel decisions: 9 touchpoints change channel, 1 is retired. Most are a channel flag, not new copy.
  • S0 S0b — Replace “Your OTP code is …” with the detailed sign-up and sign-in messages.
Week 3

Close the map's gaps

  • R2 — First-run primer — SMS, email and in-app, once ever. The highest-value missing message in the business.
  • R3 — Delivery-completed earnings reveal in-app.
  • R5 R6 — In-run prompts: running late, loaded at the pod.
  • A1a A1b A2 A3 A4 — The five admin interventions. None of them exist today.
  • C3 — Receiver delay message.
  • H7 — Host-side dwell request — the question behind two answers that already ship.
  • R9 R10 R11 — Suspension, reinstatement and account deletion. None of them exist, and the first one is the most frightening message Pinned can send.
  • H5b — Pod back online. An alert with no all-clear trains hosts to ignore alerts.
  • C1 C1b — Split the receiver's first contact: a parcel created is not a parcel moving.
Week 4

Make it compound

  • C7 — Collected → save your address.
  • P3 P4 P5 — The three prospect sequences, each capped at one message.
  • Quiet hours and the per-person frequency cap, enforced in Notifications::DispatchWorker rather than per caller.
  • A send log per recipient, so the cap and the one-event-one-message law can be checked rather than hoped for.

R7

New delivery available

The single highest-volume message Pinned sends, and the one doing the most damage.

 DefectPush
TriggerA batch offer is created
TimingOn offer and every re-offer
SourceLive code · not in the map
Routing::BatchOffer::BuildHook · Trigger
Team review · 13 OctTeam wording: “New pinned delivery available near you, Pick up {{origin}} batch distance {{km}} km, view in app.” “Near you” is kept — it is the reason to look. The money still has to lead, because the title is the only line read on a locked phone, and because four identical titles are what caused the damage in the first place.
Evidence. A runner lock screen on 9 Oct carried four identical notifications — 4m, 5m, 10m and 24m ago — all reading “New delivery available · Pickup: Superior - Gatakaini. Batch distance: 15.8 km.” Same batch, same words, stacked. By the fourth, the notification has taught the runner that Pinned pushes mean nothing.
Three causes, all in code. (1) app/services/routing/batch_offer/build.rb notifies every Runner.available — the radius and vehicle-class filters are commented out. (2) Every re-offer sends a fresh notification; nothing collapses them. (3) FcmSender sends no collapse_key / apns-collapse-id, so the OS has no way to replace the previous one.
The hook is already here and it is being wasted. A job offer is the purest variable reward in the business — payout, parcel count and distance differ every time. Put those three numbers in the title and the offer becomes the thing runners open the phone to check. Repeat the same seven words four times and it becomes the thing they swipe away.
Push · lock screentitle 29 · body 76
Pinned RunnerKsh {{payout}} · {{stops}} parcels · {{km}} kmNew delivery near you from {{origin}}. Tap to view and accept — closes {{eta}}.
Live today77 chars · 1 segment
New delivery available Pickup: Superior - Gatakaini. Batch distance: 15.8 km.
Rules
  • One live offer per batch, per runner. Collapse key batch_offer:{batch_id}; a re-offer replaces the notification in place, it never stacks.
  • Cooldown. No more than 3 offer pushes to one runner in 15 minutes, whatever the batch.
  • Radius before volume. Restore the proximity and vehicle-class filters. An offer a runner cannot take is a notification that costs trust and returns nothing.
  • Money in the title. The title is the only part read on a locked phone. Ksh first, parcels second, distance third.
  • Expiry in the body. An offer with no closing time has no reason to be opened now.
  • Silence when offline. Runners who are not online do not get offer pushes. Today they do.

06

Where this lands in the code

Nothing here needs a new messaging stack. The seams already exist.

NotificationTemplate33 seeded keys with editable bodies and a token set. Every rewrite on this site is a change to body — no migration needed.
Notifications::DispatchWorkerThe one place every channel passes through. Quiet hours, frequency caps and the send log belong here, not in each caller.
FcmSenderPosts title, body, tokens and data. No collapse key, no priority, no TTL — add all three.
Routing::BatchOffer::BuildBuilds the offer push. Radius and vehicle-class filters are commented out; the body is assembled inline rather than from a template.
MessageTemplateCampaign copy, with a validator that already refuses promotional bodies without an opt-out. The prospect sequences belong here.
MessageSenderSender identity: PINNED for SMS and WhatsApp, notifications@pinned.app for email.

Foundations

Email

Three senders, one voice

Pinned will send mail from three places — a person typing in Zoho, the Messaging Centre on pinnedflow.app, and the system itself. Only two of them can share a template, and the third should not try. This page is the rule set; the markup and the destinations live in the implementation brief beside it.

01

Where mail comes from

Three composition surfaces, and only two of them can share a template. Knowing which is which is the whole of the consistency question.

A person, in Zoho

A reply, a one-off note, a follow-up after a call. Low volume, high trust.

TemplateA Zoho Mail template, or nothing. Zoho does have Templates — Settings → Templates, inserted into a compose window — and they are right for a signature and for short standard replies.
LimitThey cannot carry the production shell. Zoho's composer sanitises what you paste: <!--[if mso]> conditional comments are stripped, the <style> block very likely goes with them — taking dark mode and every px-* class — and VML will not survive. Inline styles mostly do. The result looks close in Apple Mail and falls apart in Outlook, which is the exact failure the markup was built to prevent.

There is also no merge: a Zoho template has no way to resolve {{name}} against Pinned's data.
SoSend plain, and let the signature carry the brand. A reply from a named person should not look like a template — it should look like a person wrote it. What makes it Pinned is the vocabulary and a small, simple HTML signature, which is the one thing Zoho does reliably.
The Messaging Centre

Campaigns and outreach, composed by a human in the admin on pinnedflow.app.

TemplateThe blocks. The composer inserts them into the body and the app wraps them in the same layout at send time, so a campaign and a receipt come out of one shell. Merge tokens resolve here, against AdminApi::Fixture::Messaging.merge_tokens.
LimitNothing structural. The two things it does not have yet are the board_* endpoint and a logo on a stable HTTPS path.
SoAnything designed goes through here, not Zoho. Send the PSV pitch by hand from a mailbox and you lose the List-Unsubscribe header, you lose the per-recipient unsubscribe_url, and you put cold outreach on the same reputation as the collection codes.
The system

Collection codes, statements, approvals — ActionMailer, no human in the loop.

TemplateThe shell, as the mailer layout. app/views/layouts/mailer.html.erb becomes the shell and everything between the headline block and the footer becomes yield.
LimitNothing. This is the case the markup was written for.
SoThis is where consistency is actually won. It is the highest volume and the least negotiable, and it is a code problem rather than a discipline problem — which is why it is the one to solve first.

So: one shell for the machines, plain text for the people

Do not try to make all three render the same HTML — one of them cannot, and forcing it produces a broken Outlook render nobody tests. Make the two machine surfaces share one shell and one block library, which is where the volume is and where consistency is enforceable in code. Let the human surface be plain, carrying the brand through voice and a signature.

If a human needs to send a designed email, the answer is to send it from the Messaging Centre, not to rebuild the template in Zoho. Zoho Campaigns — a different product from Zoho Mail — does do templates, merge fields and unsubscribe handling, and is a legitimate alternative to building outreach in the admin. But pick one. Running both means two sending reputations, two unsubscribe lists, and two places a stale figure can come from.

02

What is actually configured

Rules are cheap and DNS is not. This is the live state, and two of the brief's pre-send items turn out to be build items rather than checks.

What the DNS says today, checked 10 November

The brief's checklist asks for DMARC at p=quarantine or p=reject. There is no DMARC record at all to tighten — _dmarc.pinnedflow.app returns nothing — so the first move is to publish one at p=none with a reporting address, read the reports for a fortnight to find out who is really sending as Pinned, and only then tighten.

RecordState
SPFv=spf1 include:zohomail.com ~all — Zoho and nobody else. The moment the Rails app sends through SendGrid, Postmark, SES or Mailgun, those messages fail SPF. Either route system mail through Zoho's SMTP too, or add the provider before the first send — not after.
DKIMLive for Zoho (zmail._domainkey). Any second sending provider needs its own selector published.
DMARCAbsent. Not p=none — absent. Nothing is being reported, so nobody knows what is currently sending as pinnedflow.app.
Subdomainsmail., marketing., news. and send. all return nothing. The three-class separation is a plan, not a configuration.
The trade-offSPF naming only Zoho makes Zoho SMTP the cheapest path to all-three-pass on day one. It is also the path that makes class separation hardest, because one tenant's reputation is shared. Worth deciding deliberately rather than by default.
03

Three sending classes

They differ for legal and deliverability reasons, not visual ones. Same shell for all three.

Transactional

ExamplesCollection code, ready for collection, collected, host statement.
UnsubscribeNo — an unsubscribe link does not belong on a message somebody needs.
FromPinned <notifications@pinnedflow.app>
Domainmail.pinnedflow.app, never throttled.

Lifecycle / service

ExamplesA new Pod, a new feature, a change to how something works.
UnsubscribeYes, plus a preferences link.
FromPinned <notifications@pinnedflow.app>
DomainA separate marketing subdomain.

Prospecting

ExamplesPSV operators, cold business senders, the pitch on this site.
UnsubscribeYes, and prominent.
FromPinned Flow <hello@pinnedflow.app> — monitored, never noreply@.
DomainA third subdomain, warmed slowly.
04

The rules

01

Separate the classes by subdomain, not by good intentions

A complaint about a cold pitch must not be able to delay the collection-code email that lets somebody open a Pod. That is the entire reason for the split, and it is a DNS job rather than a copy job.

02

Never noreply@ on a class whose success is a reply

The pitch ends on “Book a 15-minute walkthrough… or call”. A SACCO manager's first instinct is to hit reply. Prospecting sends from a mailbox somebody reads.

03

The subject line carries the fact — on transactional

The amount, the date, the decision. This rule is scoped to transactional and it was not before. Outreach earns its open with a hook instead: “Still on your Nairobi shelf tonight” carries no fact at all, and is right.

04

Name which code it is

The collection code is read at a counter and is never withheld. The handover code is given to a runner at a door and is held until what is owed clears. Never PIN — that is the runner app's device unlock. Never OTP — that is our word, not theirs.

05

Figures come from the live board, pulled the morning you send, and dated

Never typed by hand, which is how a stale number reaches a customer. No ratings. The retired 480 / 12,000+ / 4.8 figures stay retired.

06

One call to action, and it carries the link

No gating, no “reply to find out”, no keyword barrier. The address travels with the call.

07

DM Mono is reserved, and in email it is also a signal

Codes, prices, timestamps and IDs only. In an inbox it is the fastest way a receiver tells a real Pinned message from a copy of one.

08

Flow lane colours only

#C8005A #C0DB86 #7AAF2A #E8520A #2B2B2B. PinnedOS Blue never appears on a Flow asset.

09

UKO WAPI? is rationed

It belongs to the sign-up message and nowhere else in the sender journey.

05

Why the markup looks like 2004

Do not modernise it. Each of these is load-bearing, and a developer who tidies one of them breaks a client they are not testing in.

WhatWhy it is there
Tables and inline CSSOutlook renders with Word's engine. No flexbox, no grid, no external stylesheet.
XHTML doctype + the MSO blockWithout it Outlook scales everything by its own DPI.
The VML <v:roundrect>Without it the magenta button is a blue underlined link in Outlook.
mso-line-height-rule: exactlyOn every text cell, or Outlook ignores line height.
The hidden preheader divThe grey line beside the subject. Left empty, clients scrape the first visible text instead.
Dark mode, and two logosGmail and Apple Mail invert colours on their own and do it badly. Blocks 3 and 4 deliberately do not invert — anthracite and Locker Green are brand grounds, not theme colours.
DM Sans only renders in Apple Mail and iOSGmail and Outlook fall back to Arial. That is expected. Never set headlines as images to force the font — it costs accessibility and spam score.

07

Before the first send

Publish a DMARC record

There is none. Start at p=none with a reporting address, read a fortnight of reports, then tighten to p=quarantine.

Authorise every sender in SPF

Today it is Zoho only. Decide whether system mail goes through Zoho SMTP or a provider, and publish the record before the first send.

Logos on a stable HTTPS path

Then point {{asset_base}} at the folder.

List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post

On bulk sends. unsubscribe_url must be unique per recipient — a shared link unsubscribes nobody, and Gmail and Yahoo require a unique one for the one-click header.

A text/plain alternative on every send

Missing it costs deliverability on its own.

The board_* endpoint

A small endpoint or cached read of the /vhost board. Until it exists those four tokens have nothing behind them.

Render it in five places

Gmail web, Gmail Android, Apple Mail iOS, Outlook desktop, and Gmail dark mode.

BIMI is a 2027 item

The logo beside the subject line needs DMARC enforcement plus a Verified Mark Certificate, which needs a registered trademark and runs about $1,000 a year. Do the DMARC work now for its own sake.

How Pinned speaks

Nine principles, a warmth layer, and a contract every message signs

Clarity is not a style. It is the same four questions answered in the same order, every time, in every channel. Warmth is not a style either — it is the four or five moments in a journey where somebody has something at stake, and we show that we know it.

01

The principles

01

Say what happened, first

The event goes in the first four words. Not a greeting, not the brand, not a preamble. A person reading a lock screen in the sun gets one line — spend it on the fact.

Write thisDelivered. Wanjiru collected PN-4K7Q2 at Lavington, 13:42.
Not thisGreetings from Pinned! We are pleased to inform you that your parcel has now been successfully delivered.
02

One message, one action

If a message cannot name a single next step, it is not a message, it is a thought. Two actions in one SMS means neither gets done.

Write thisCode: 840 213 — show it at the counter.
Not thisYou can collect it, or request door delivery, or extend your storage, or contact the sender.
03

Name names

The pod, the street, the runner, the amount, the hour. Specifics are what turn a notification into information. Generic words are the sound of a system talking to itself.

Write thisYour parcel from Sokoni Stores is at Shell Kileleshwa, open until 21:00.
Not thisYour parcel has arrived at the designated collection point.
04

Numbers are facts, never threats

Charges, deadlines and returns are stated once, calmly, with the way to avoid them in the same breath. Pinned never raises its voice at someone who owes it fifty shillings.

Write thisStorage has started: Ksh 50/hr. It stops the moment you collect.
Not thisFINAL NOTICE! Storage charges are ACCRUING. Immediate collection is required to avoid further penalties.
05

Never make someone open an app to learn something a line could tell them

'Open the app for details' is a tax we charge the customer for our own convenience. If it fits in the SMS, it goes in the SMS.

Write thisNow arriving about 14:20 — the rain has closed Ngong Road.
Not thisThere has been an update to your delivery. Open the app to see the details.
06

The code is the hero

Codes, OTPs and tracking references sit alone on their own line, in DM Mono, never mid-sentence and never split across a line break. The brand reserves mono for exactly this.

Write thisCollection code:
840 213
Not thisPlease use the one-time-password 840213 at the locker to retrieve your item.
07

Kenyan, not American

Collect, not pick up. Ksh, written one way everywhere. 24-hour clock. Petrol station. Estate. Ordinary Kenyan English, written the way the counter staff speak.

Write thisOpen until 21:00. Ksh 50/hr after Friday.
Not thisAvailable until 9 PM. A fee of KES 50.00 per hour shall apply thereafter.
08

Apologise once, then fix it

One short apology, then the new time or the new plan. Repeated sorries read as a system that knows it is unreliable and expects to stay that way.

Write thisSorry — late. Now about 14:20, and we text when it lands.
Not thisWe sincerely apologise for the inconvenience. We apologise again for any disruption this may have caused.
09

First contact names the pain, not the feature

The first message any audience gets has one job beyond its own: say what Pinned takes off their hands. A runner hears “six minutes to jobs”, a host hears what the pod pays, a sender hears the end of ringing people to ask where things are. Features come later, to people who already care.

Write thisKaribu Pinned, Wanjiru — your answer to UKO WAPI?
Not thisWelcome to Pinned. Your sign-up code is 840 213.
02

The warmth layer

Round one was correct and cold. It answered what happened, what do I do and never why should I care. Warmth is not decoration and it is not exclamation marks — it is the four or five places in a journey where a person has something at stake, and we show that we know it. Everywhere else, warmth is noise.

01

Use their name where there is a person, not a parcel

At the start, at a milestone, at a failure and anywhere money moves. Not on routine status.

Write thisWanjiru, your refund of Ksh 1,250 for Parcel PN-4K7Q2 has been processed.
Not thisHi Wanjiru! Parcel PN-4K7Q2 is now in transit, Wanjiru.
02

Name the stake, not the record

“Your first job”, “someone has sent you something”, “your account is open”. The sentence that tells them why this message is about them and not about our database.

Write thisCongratulations Brian on your first job.
Not thisBatch B-2291 status: accepted.
03

Acknowledge the wait or the effort — once

A single line of recognition where someone has given us time, documents or patience. One line. Never two, never in every message.

Write thisThank you Brian for submitting your documents. We appreciate your patience.
Not thisWe appreciate your patience. Thank you for bearing with us. Sorry for the wait.
04

Celebrate the real milestone, ignore the fake one

Approval, first run, first delivery, the week they beat their own record. Not “your parcel has moved to the next stage”.

Write thisDelivery complete · Ksh 640 earned. Keep going — your next job is waiting.
Not thisCongratulations! Your parcel has successfully entered the sorting process!
05

Assume the kind reason

People go offline because the battery died, miss a drop-off because the day ran away with them, and fail to collect because they are at work. Write as if that is true, because it usually is.

Write thisIf that wasn't you, open the app and tap Go online.
Not thisYou have failed to remain available as required.
06

Leave the door open on a no

Every rejection, denial, suspension or failure names the way back in the same message — and says it in a sentence a person would actually say out loud.

Write thisIf you think this is wrong, you can appeal here.
Not thisThis decision is final. Contact support for further information.

Where the warmth is allowed to go

Warmth spent everywhere is warmth nobody feels. Each lane gets a short list of moments, and the messages in between stay plain.

Senders

Sign-up, handover code, delivered, refund, return. Five warm moments in a journey of a dozen messages.

Receivers

The first message — someone has sent you something — and the collected message. The ladder in between stays plain; warmth inside a charging notice reads as sarcasm.

Runners

Welcome, documents received, approved or not approved, first job, every job completion, suspension and its reversal. The runner lane carries the most warmth because it carries the most at stake.

Hosts

Activation and the weekly settlement. A host is a business, and a business wants the number and the facts — but the first week and the money are still human moments.

The positioning line

Uko wapi?

The question every delivery in Nairobi collapses into. The sender rings the rider, the receiver rings the sender, the shop rings everybody, and nobody has an answer. Pinned's product is the answer — a code, a map and a pod instead of four phone calls.

First contact, once per person

The sign-up message, and nowhere else in the sender journey. A line this good survives exactly as long as it stays rare.

Marketing, freely

Groups, print, the landing, the merch. Repetition is the job there.

Never in a transactional message

A storage charge, a failed delivery or a collection code is not the moment for a slogan. It reads as a company enjoying itself while the customer has a problem.

Never translated into English in copy

“Your answer to where are you?” is a different, worse sentence. It works because it is the words people actually use.

03

The clarity contract

Every Pinned message answers these four questions, in this order. A message that cannot answer the third should not be sent.

1. What happened

The event, in the first line. Past tense for facts, present for states.

2. What it means for me

Only if it is not obvious. Money, deadlines and location always qualify.

3. What I do now

Exactly one action. If there is no action, say 'nothing to do until then'.

4. What I need to do it

The code, the link, the address, the hour. Nothing that is not needed for the action.

04

The hook, applied

Trigger, action, variable reward, investment. Pinned already has genuine variable rewards — the runner's payout, the host's weekly figure, the proof photo. None of them are currently where the person looks.

Trigger

External, then internal

Every Pinned message is an external trigger. It earns its place by eventually becoming an internal one: the runner who checks the phone for the next offer, the receiver who thinks 'the code came by SMS last time'. Triggers that fire without information are how the internal one is destroyed.

Action

The simplest thing that moves the parcel

One tap, one code, one walk-in. Measure every message by how many actions it asks for; the answer is always one. If the action needs the app, the message carries the deep link; if it does not, the message carries everything.

Variable reward

What differs every time

The runner's payout, stop count and distance. The host's weekly figure. The sender's proof photo and the name of the person who signed. The receiver's free-storage countdown they beat. Pinned has genuine variable rewards already — they are currently buried.

Investment

What makes the next one better

A saved address, a renewed document, a rating, a branch default, a repeat receiver. Each one is asked for at the moment immediately after a success, never before one, and never more than once.

Senders

TriggerBooking confirmation lands before they have left the pod counter.
ActionDrop the parcel, or tap once to send a runner instead.
RewardProof of delivery with a name, a time and a photo — the moment that makes the next send automatic.
InvestmentSaved addresses and repeat receivers; the second send is three taps.

Receivers

Trigger'Your parcel from Sokoni Stores is at Shell Kileleshwa.'
ActionWalk in, read the code, collect.
RewardFree-storage countdown they beat, and a door-delivery escape hatch they did not expect.
InvestmentSaving the delivery address turns the next parcel into a doorstep drop.

Runners

TriggerAn offer push carrying the money, the distance and the clock.
ActionOne tap to accept.
RewardVariable by design — payout, stop count and distance differ every time; the earnings reveal at completion is the hit.
InvestmentDocuments kept current, rating, completed runs — all of it buys better batches.

Hosts

Trigger'A runner is 12 minutes away for 4 parcels.'
ActionHave them at the counter, scan, release the code.
RewardThe weekly settlement figure — the number they open the email for.
InvestmentBranch defaults, cut-off times and handover habits that make the station faster each week.

Admin interventions

TriggerA human decision, pushed to the person it affects within seconds.
ActionAccept the new state, or say what is happening.
RewardBeing told early, before it becomes their problem.
InvestmentRunners who are told the truth about reassignments keep taking offers.

Prospects

TriggerA reply in the group, or an SMS an hour after they checked a price.
ActionGet a quote. Ten seconds, no account.
RewardA real price and a real time, instantly — not a callback.
InvestmentThe quote keeps their route; signing up is one field.

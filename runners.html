01

What they are here for

The job they hire Pinned for

Fill the day with jobs worth taking, and get paid without chasing anyone.

Every message on this page is judged against that sentence. If it does not help with it, or protect it, it should not be sent.

The hook
TriggerAn offer push carrying the money, the distance and the clock.
ActionOne tap to accept.
RewardVariable by design — payout, stop count and distance differ every time; the earnings reveal at completion is the hit.
InvestmentDocuments kept current, rating, completed runs — all of it buys better batches.

Pinned · notifications@pinned.appYour first Pinned run — what happens at each stop
1. Collect. Scan each parcel at the pod or counter. The app will not let you leave with an unscanned parcel, and that protects you. 2. Custody. From the scan until hand-over, the parcel is yours. If anything looks wrong — open box, wet, wrong label — photograph it in the app before you move. 3. Hand over. The receiver reads you their code. You enter it. Never accept a code sent to you by anyone else, and never read the code out loud first. 4. Get paid. Earnings land every {{deadline}} on the M-Pesa number on your account. If a receiver is not there: wait 5 minutes, call from the app, then mark 'not available'. Do not leave a parcel with a neighbour.

Offers

R7

New delivery available

The single highest-volume message Pinned sends, and the one doing the most damage.

 DefectPush
TriggerA batch offer is created
TimingOn offer and every re-offer
SourceLive code · not in the map
Routing::BatchOffer::BuildHook · Trigger
Evidence. A runner lock screen on 9 Oct carried four identical notifications — 4m, 5m, 10m and 24m ago — all reading “New delivery available · Pickup: Superior - Gatakaini. Batch distance: 15.8 km.” Same batch, same words, stacked. By the fourth, the notification has taught the runner that Pinned pushes mean nothing.
Three causes, all in code. (1) app/services/routing/batch_offer/build.rb notifies every Runner.available — the radius and vehicle-class filters are commented out. (2) Every re-offer sends a fresh notification; nothing collapses them. (3) FcmSender sends no collapse_key / apns-collapse-id, so the OS has no way to replace the previous one.
The hook is already here and it is being wasted. A job offer is the purest variable reward in the business — payout, parcel count and distance differ every time. Put those three numbers in the title and the offer becomes the thing runners open the phone to check. Repeat the same seven words four times and it becomes the thing they swipe away.
Push · lock screentitle 29 · body 63
Pinned RunnerKsh {{payout}} · {{stops}} parcels · {{km}} km{{origin}} → {{dest}}. Tap to accept — this offer closes {{eta}}.
Live today77 chars · 1 segment
New delivery available Pickup: Superior - Gatakaini. Batch distance: 15.8 km.
Rules
  • One live offer per batch, per runner. Collapse key batch_offer:{batch_id}; a re-offer replaces the notification in place, it never stacks.
  • Cooldown. No more than 3 offer pushes to one runner in 15 minutes, whatever the batch.
  • Radius before volume. Restore the proximity and vehicle-class filters. An offer a runner cannot take is a notification that costs trust and returns nothing.
  • Money in the title. The title is the only part read on a locked phone. Ksh first, parcels second, distance third.
  • Expiry in the body. An offer with no closing time has no reason to be opened now.
  • Silence when offline. Runners who are not online do not get offer pushes. Today they do.

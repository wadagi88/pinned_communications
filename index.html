Why this exists

Evidence

Four notifications, one job, twenty minutes

A Pinned Runner lock screen on the morning of 9 October: four notifications at 4, 5, 10 and 24 minutes, every one of them reading New delivery available — Pickup: Superior - Gatakaini. Batch distance: 15.8 km. Same batch. Same words. No payout, no parcel count, no closing time, nothing to tell the fourth from the first.

This is not a copy problem, it is a system that does not know what it has already said. Everything on this site exists to stop that specific lock screen from happening again.

The philosophy

Great products are not an accident

A delivery business is judged on the messages far more often than on the deliveries. Most customers touch Pinned four times a year and read our SMS six times a week. The messages are the product for everyone who is not holding a parcel.

Clarity and intuitiveness are not a tone of voice. They are a set of decisions taken once, written down, and then enforced in code: one event one message, one action per message, four rungs then silence, the code on its own line.

Round two · 13 October

The team marked every channel

Three documents and an annotated screenshot came back: which channel to keep, which to drop, which message to move, and four that do not exist at all. Nine touchpoints change channel, one is retired outright, and the copy follows the team's own wording wherever they wrote it.

The other verdict was that round one was correct and cold — it never answered why should I care. Hence the warmth layer, and an SMS journey on every lane page so the cost of a channel decision is visible before it is taken.

01

How to read this

Live

A template exists in NotificationTemplate and fires. The copy here is a rewrite of what is already sending.

Partial

Something fires, but it is missing a channel the map asks for, a rung in its ladder, or the point of the message.

Not built

The map asks for it, or the hook needs it, and nothing sends it today.

Defect

It fires, and what it does is wrong — a duplicate, a wrong token, or a notification that damages trust each time it lands.

Character counts are measured with the longest realistic values filled in, not with the tokens left empty. An SMS that fits “Jay” and breaks on “Wanjiru” has not been written.

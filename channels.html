What each channel is for

Six channels, eight laws

A channel is a job, not a copy of the message. “(sms, email, in-app)” in the map means three different jobs — not the same sentence three times.

01

Channel doctrine

SMS

Lands anywhere, costs money, cannot be taken back

Use forAnything that must reach a person who is not in the app: arrival, codes, money, deadlines, failures.
Never forProgress updates, thank-yous, anything a push or an in-app card can carry.
Rules
  • 160 GSM-7 characters is one segment. Two segments are allowed when the message carries money, a deadline and an action — and once per audience for first contact, where the second segment buys the reason to care.
  • Sender ID PINNED on everything.
  • No link shorteners the customer cannot read. The domain is the trust signal.
  • Non-urgent SMS is held between 21:00 and 06:30 EAT.
Push

Free, instant, and the easiest thing in the world to turn off

Use forLive operational state while the app is where the action happens: offers, route changes, ETAs, in-run prompts.
Never forAnything the runner must still be told if they never open the phone. Push is not delivery, it is a nudge.
Rules
  • Every push carries a collapse key. A second push about the same thing replaces the first; it never stacks.
  • Maximum 3 pushes to one person in 15 minutes, across all types.
  • Title ≤ 45 characters, body ≤ 110 — past that, the lock screen truncates and the sentence is wasted.
  • Never push to someone who is offline or off shift.
In-app

The record. Everything that happened, in order, forever

Use forEvery event, without exception, including ones that also went out by SMS.
Never forBeing the only channel for money, deadlines or a code. If it matters off-app, it goes off-app too.
Rules
  • The in-app entry is written first; the SMS is a compression of it.
  • Earnings and settlement figures always appear in the same place, in the same shape.
  • Nothing in the in-app feed disappears. A customer should be able to prove what we told them.
Email

The channel that can hold a process or a document

Use forOnboarding primers, settlement statements, approvals and rejections with detail, anything with an attachment.
Never forUrgency. Nobody in this business reads email on the road.
Rules
  • The subject line carries the fact — the amount, the date, the decision.
  • From notifications@pinned.app, display name Pinned.
  • One-time explanatory emails say in the message that they are one-time.
WhatsApp

Two-way, where Kenyan commerce actually happens

Use forEnquiries, group replies, and anything where the answer is a conversation.
Never forAutomated transactional blasts. The moment it feels like SMS, the thread gets blocked.
Rules
  • Replies come from a human within 5 minutes in working hours, or the channel is not open.
  • Template-initiated messages only where the person has written to us first.
  • Priced like SMS. It is not free, and it is not a dumping ground.
Call

The exception path, by a person

Use forA failed delivery with something valuable in it, a dispute, a safety issue.
Never forEverything else. Pinned does not make automated calls.
Rules
  • A call is always preceded by the SMS that explains why someone is about to ring.
  • Never an automated voice. If it is worth a call, it is worth a person.
02

The eight laws

These are the decisions that stop the estate drifting back into noise. Each one is enforceable in Notifications::DispatchWorker rather than trusted to whoever writes the next template.

01

One event, one message

A single thing that happened produces a single message per person. Two templates describing the same moment is a bug, not thoroughness. Today the hand-over fires twice and the batch offer fires as many times as the system re-offers.

02

Channel ladder, not channel broadcast

In-app always. SMS only when the action happens away from the app. Push only when the app is the destination. Email only when it is a document or a process. The map's '(sms, email, in-app)' means a ladder of three different jobs, not the same sentence three times.

03

Collapse before you send

Every push carries a collapse key built from what it is about — batch_offer:{batch_id}, parcel:{code}. The second notification replaces the first. Four identical notifications on a lock screen is the single clearest evidence of a system that does not know what it has already said.

04

Quiet hours, 21:00 – 06:30 EAT

Non-urgent SMS and push are held until morning. Urgent is defined narrowly and in code: a delivery in progress, a collection code, a security event, money leaving an account. Nothing else is urgent at 23:00.

05

Four rungs, then silence

Reminder ladders escalate by specificity — neutral, specific, consequence, final — and stop. Pinned does not send a fifth message, and it never sends one after the final notice. The 6-hour dwell reminder is a fifth rung at the wrong end and should go.

06

One promotional message per person per week

Across every sequence, every lane. Transactional messages are uncapped, but each one must pass the clarity contract — a transactional message that fails it is promotional with extra steps.

07

Every escape hatch is in the message

If there is an alternative — door delivery, redeliver, extend, decline — it appears in the message that creates the problem, not in a later one. Rung 2 of the storage ladder converts because the escape hatch is right there.

08

Internal words stay internal

Dwell, batch, OTP, eligibility, heartbeat, float, SLA, vhost. None of these reach a customer or a runner. The customer-facing word is listed beside each one in the glossary.

03

The escalation ladder

Every reminder sequence uses the same four rungs. Tone escalates by specificity, never by volume, and nothing follows the fourth.

Rung 1 · neutral

State the fact and the opening hours. Mention no charge, because none applies.

Your parcel is still at Shell Kileleshwa, open until 21:00.
Rung 2 · specific

Name the deadline, the rate that follows it, and the alternative.

Free storage ends Fri 18:00. After that: Ksh 50/hr.
Rung 3 · consequence

The charge has started. Say how to stop it in the same line.

Ksh 50/hr, Ksh 1,250 so far. It stops the moment you collect.
Rung 4 · final

A fact already in motion, a total, and a human to talk to. Then silence.

Last day: it goes back to the sender today.
04

The storage clock

The one fact that most copy on this site depends on, and the easiest to get wrong.

Free storage is 24 hours, and the clock is not the wall clock

A parcel gets 24 countable hours of free storage. The clock does not run between 19:00 and 08:00, and it does not run while the pod is closed. A parcel that lands at 15:00 on Monday is therefore free until the middle of Wednesday, not until Tuesday afternoon.

Never state a duration to a customer

“24 hours of free storage” is true in our database and wrong on their phone. Every message states {{deadline}} — a day and a time the person can plan around.

Schedule the ladder on the storage clock

Rungs fire at 12 countable hours left, 4 countable hours left, and at expiry. Because they are scheduled on the clock rather than on elapsed time, no rung can arrive overnight or while the pod the parcel is sitting in is shut.

Charging starts when the clock does, and stops when they collect

The paused hours are free hours. If a customer disputes a charge, the answer has to be a line they can check themselves — which is why the amount so far appears in rung 3.

An extension is granted in hours and communicated as a time

Hosts approve “+24 hours”; receivers are told “now runs to Thursday 11:00”. The two numbers are never the same, and only one of them belongs in an SMS.

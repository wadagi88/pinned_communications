02

The SMS journey

Every moment in order, and the channel that carries it. Highlighted rows cost an SMS: 4 on a journey that works, 8 more only when something goes wrong. A row numbered + is one of those; everything unhighlighted is a deliberate decision not to spend an SMS.

Four SMS on a journey that works — and the receiver has no app

Every receiver rule is the inverse of the sender rules. There is no in-app to fall back on, so SMS carries everything — which is exactly why the ladder has to stop at four rungs. A parcel that has been created and a parcel that is moving are two different messages.

1Parcel created for themSMSSomeone is sending you a parcel. Nothing to do yetThe notification. Informational, no action, no code.
2Runner collects itSMSOn its way, with a runner and an ETAThe expected parcel. Optional for a pod drop, required for a door delivery.

Rules

  • Never open a receiver's first message with 'Your parcel {{code}}'. A code they have never seen is not a greeting.

C3

Running late

A new time, a reason, and when the next message comes.

 Not builtSMS
TriggerPromised delivery time passes while the parcel is in transit
TimingAt the moment the promise breaks
SourceMap · Receiver 2
Hook · Trigger
SMS146 chars · 1 segment
Your parcel from {{from}} is running late — now about {{eta}}. {{reason}}. We text again the moment it lands at {{station}}.
Rules
  • Never 'we apologise for any inconvenience caused'. Give a new time instead; that is the apology.
  • Promise the next message. A delay is tolerable when you know when you will next hear something.

Rules
  • Rung 1 does not mention the rate. Nothing is being charged yet, so nothing about charging is said.
  • Retire the 6-hour reminder. It arrives while people are still at work and teaches them we message for no reason.
  • Scheduled on the storage clock, not the wall clock, so it can never fire overnight or while the pod is shut.

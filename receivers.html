02

The SMS journey

Every moment in order, and the channel that carries it. Highlighted rows cost an SMS: 4 on a journey that works, 11 more only when something goes wrong. A row numbered + is one of those; everything unhighlighted is a deliberate decision not to spend an SMS.

Four SMS on a journey that works — and the receiver has no app

Every receiver rule is the inverse of the sender rules. There is no in-app to fall back on, so SMS carries everything, and each message hands over to publicchoicepage.netlify.app — which is why the two have to sound like one company. First contact branches on who sent it and whether anything is owed; arrival branches between a door and a pod.

Rules
  • Free is named before the paid option, in every variant. It is the single clearest signal that Pinned is not trying to extract something from a person who did not choose to be a customer.
  • The money line never appears when there is nothing to pay. Four variants exist so that three of them can stay short.
  • No code, no deadline, nothing else to act on. The link is the only action.
  • The link stands on its own line with no “click below”. The page is the invitation.

SMS143 chars · 1 segment
Good news {{name}} — {{sender_name}} has sent you a parcel through Pinned. It's at {{station}}, heading to you now. Track it: {{track_url}}
Rules
  • A different link. /p/ is the choice page; /track/ is the tracking page. Sending a pre-paid receiver to a page with a price on it is how you generate a support call.
  • The address is still editable on the tracking page — the sender set it, and the person living there is the one who knows if it is wrong.

C8

Your runner can't find you

Somebody is standing in the wrong place. Fix it in one tap, without blaming anyone.

 Not builtSMS
TriggerThe runner is at the address and cannot reach the receiver
TimingOn the runner reporting it
SourceReceiver page · 27 Oct
Hook · Action
Team review · 13 OctOnly the live page has this. Neither the Communication Map nor the receiver pack includes it, and it is the moment a delivery is most likely to fail. The page's wording is used almost as written, because it is already the voice.
Warmth“Moved on?” is the whole trick. It assumes the ordinary explanation instead of asking why they are not where they said.
SMS136 chars · 1 segment
{{runner}} is at {{spot}} and can't find you at {{dest}}. Moved on? Tell us where you are now: {{track_url}}
Rules
  • The page states the limit before anyone hits it: one update per delivery, up to 3 km from your first address. That sentence belongs on the page, not in the SMS — the SMS has one job, which is to get them to the page.
  • The fallback is generous and it is said out loud on the page: if we can't reach you at your door, it returns to the branch after a 20-min wait — no charge. A failed delivery that costs the receiver nothing is the reason this message can stay calm.
  • Never “you were not available”. The runner is the one who cannot find them, and that is how the sentence is built.
